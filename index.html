The problem, in numbers

India's plate is heavy on carbs and light on protein, and it shows.

A generic calorie count misses what matters most for Indian health: where the calories come from, and where the body stores them.

62%of daily calories come from carbohydrates, one of the highest shares in the world1
12%of calories come from protein. Most of it is plant protein from cereals and pulses1
21states and UTs exceed the national limit of under 5% of energy from added sugar1
351MIndians have abdominal obesity, 39.5% of adults2
101Mlive with diabetes, and another 136 million with prediabetes2
56.4%of India's total disease burden is attributed to unhealthy diets3
Diabetes, 11.4%Prediabetes, 15.3%Neither

More than 1 in 4 Indian adults already has diabetes or prediabetes.2

The same nationwide data links high-carbohydrate diets to a higher chance of new type 2 diabetes. That's why Dabba tracks your carb share against ICMR's guidance (no more than 45% of calories from cereals and millets3), not just your calorie total.

BMI was calibrated on European bodies

A "healthy" 24 elsewhere is overweight in Dabba.

A WHO expert consultation found that many Asians face high risk of type 2 diabetes and heart disease below the global overweight cut-off of 25, with risk starting between 22 and 25 depending on the population.4 Dabba uses the Indian consensus cut-offs, so the warning comes earlier.

A forecast that tells you the truth

Dabba shows where your habits lead, before the scale does.

Eating 400 kcal over targetEating to target"3,500 kcal rule" (to target)

Weight responds to a change in eating slowly, with a half-time of about a year.5 The popular "3,500 kcal per pound" rule ignores that, and overestimates weight loss.6 Dabba simulates your body day by day instead: your calorie burn falls as your weight falls, and each kilogram is split into fat and lean tissue by how much fat you carry.7

Then it learns from you. Each time you weigh in, a regression compares the forecast with your scale and estimates the calories your log is missing, such as oil, chai and second helpings. If your path drifts away from your goal, or you're eating too little to be healthy, Dabba tells you on screen.

Rewarding balance, not starvation

The Thali Score: 100 points a day, and skipping meals loses them.

Dabba's calorie points peak within 7% of your target and fall to zero 35% away, on either side, so eating far too little costs as many points as eating far too much. The leaderboard rewards people who eat well and log consistently, not the ones who starve.

Daily scores turn into XP, streaks, 10 levels from Chai Rookie to Dabba Legend, and 20 badges, including Ekadashi Keeper and Chai in Check.
